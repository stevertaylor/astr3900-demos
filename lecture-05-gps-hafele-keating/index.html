Two Effects, One Clock

Every moving, elevated clock is pulled two ways at once: special-relativistic time dilation slows it down, gravitational redshift speeds it up. Pick a real scenario and watch the two effects race.

Real GPS satellites orbit at ≈20,200 km altitude. At that altitude the gravitational (GR) effect dominates the kinematic (SR) effect, and the satellite's on-board clock must be corrected by:

net ≈ +38.5 µs / day

(−7.2 µs/day from its orbital speed, +45.7 µs/day from being higher up a shallower potential well — built into every GPS receiver's firmware.)

Photon Orbits and Light Bending

Launch light past a Schwarzschild mass: find the capture threshold b = √27 M, then test the deflection 4M/b.
Model: exact equatorial null geodesics of the Schwarzschild metric, G = c = 1, lengths in units of M, drawn to scale as x = r cos φ, y = r sin φ. Orbit equation u″ = 3Mu2 − u (u = 1/r), RK4 in φ. Only the clock is sped up: photons move at their true rate in the distant observer’s time t, with 1 s of animation = – M of t (1 M = 4.93 μs for one solar mass). By that clock a captured photon never quite reaches r = 2M; runs stop at 2.02M.

Rays are launched together from one plane far to the right, so at any moment the photons mark a wavefront. Watch it wrap round the mass.

Predict, then run

Which rays will be captured? Of the escaping rays, which one is bent the most, and roughly how far round the mass does it go?

Every ray with |b| < √27 M ≈ 5.2M is captured (here 0.8–5.0M, 12 rays). The 5.4M rays escape after sweeping ≈ 347° in φ, a deflection of ≈ 167°: the closer b is to √27 M, the more turns.

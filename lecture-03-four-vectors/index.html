Doppler shift from a Lorentz transformation

Frame convention. O is the source rest frame. In O′ the source moves with velocity +β along +x′. We transform photon four-momentum components from O to O′.

The same set of rays is drawn in both panels. Equally spaced directions in the source-rest slice are Lorentz-transformed into O′. The clustering toward +x′ is relativistic aberration, not a change in the speed of light. Forward rays are also blueshifted and backward rays redshifted, which is the Doppler factor D.

From aberration to Doppler boosting

For an isotropic source, aberration compresses solid angle in the forward direction. Doppler shift and arrival-time effects then strengthen the received beam.

D2 is the angular concentration for a single isotropic flash because dΩ′ = dΩ/D2. D3 describes the received photon rate per solid angle for continuous emission. Multiplying once more by the photon-energy shift gives the bolometric luminosity per solid angle ∝ D4. A monochromatic spectral intensity at fixed observing frequency has a different transformation law. On the linear scale the backward lobe all but vanishes at high β; switch to the log scale, which always shows enough decades to include it.
